One-color inventory pixel icons: terminology and algorithm direction

Independent research, October 8, 2026. Research only; no production changes. Reviewed supplied 65-item corpus, previous experiments, structural prototype previews, and primary research / artist sources.

Recommended target: a 1-bit pixel glyph: one flat ink color, binary transparency, a filled body, and a small number of deliberate negative-space features. Optimize spatial structure and recognition, rather than faithfully reproducing tone. Accept the supplied messy AI input as the operating condition.

What the style is called

TermUse / limitation
1-bit pixel icons / monochrome pixel iconsThe clearest search terms used by actual RPG asset packs. Binary shape can mean two opaque colors or one color plus transparency; specify the latter.
Filled / solid glyph, pictogram, silhouette with interior cutoutsDescribes the visual target more exactly than “flat,” which also includes multicolor artwork. A pure silhouette has no inner features.
Negative-space icon / cutout icon / knockout detailDescribes transparency carrying the interior information. These are descriptive terms rather than a measured pixel-art genre ranking.
StencilUseful metaphor, but a physical stencil normally needs bridges to prevent islands falling out. A digital icon can freely contain disconnected ink, such as the two ends of a necklace.
Binary image abstraction / two-tone stylization / non-photorealistic renderingThe most productive research terms for algorithms. “Binarization” alone predominantly returns thresholding and scanned-document methods.

Kenney’s 1-Bit Pack is explicitly a 16×16 RPG/roguelike asset collection with 1,078 files; its author’s itch page describes monochrome and transparent variants. GingerCharacters’ 143 RPG 1-Bit Game Icons directly matches the target: 16×16, pure white, transparent PNGs, covering weapons, armor, and items. These establish a recognizable convention. Neither gives comparative evidence that one rendering substyle is the most common.

Lorc’s breastplate at Game-icons.net offers white/transparent and black/transparent versions. This is a useful filled-glyph reference, but is vector artwork at much greater spatial capacity; directly reducing it to 16 pixels is a different task.

Evidence most relevant to conversion

SourceFindingApplication here
Mould & Grant, Stylized Black and White Images from Photographs (2008)Separates coherent base regions from local high-contrast detail. Global thresholds miss features; local thresholds amplify noise. Uses graph cuts / energy minimization and component cleanup.Use alpha and inferred border removal as the body, then a separate sparse detail map. A graph-cut labeler with a strong body prior is a credible next comparison. The paper does not validate 16×16 AI inventory sprites.
Kang, Lee & Chui, Coherent Line Drawing (2007)Flow-guided anisotropic filtering joins salient lines while suppressing fragmented noise.At 16×16, adapt the principle to short, directionally supported valleys or boundaries. Full smoothing across many pixels can erase an entire handle or armor neckline. This is a heuristic adaptation, not the published algorithm.
Tangent-Based Binary Image Abstraction (2017)Combines tangent-flow feature enhancement, contrast reduction in low-contrast regions, and binary output.Provides a second structural-abstraction family worth benchmarking. Its photo-oriented filtering sizes and cleanup cannot be copied directly onto a 16-pixel sprite.
Tomasi & Manduchi, Bilateral Filtering (1998)Smooths nearby, similar colors while retaining substantial boundaries.Useful preprocessing when AI noise is subtle. It cannot decide whether a sharp boundary is a seam, shadow, or texture; prior corpus results already show bilateral + threshold is insufficient.
Chen & Peng, Topology-Preserving Downsampling (2024)Tracks foreground components and holes separately. Similarity and topology are different objectives. Uses complementary 8-connected foreground / 4-connected background.Count fragmentation and hole loss explicitly. Preserve original transparent holes where possible. Do not claim full topology preservation when intentionally creating new holes for detail.
Liu et al., Data-Driven Iconification (2016)Combines salient-region detection, shape matching, and graph-cut stitching from an icon repository.Shows the distinct route toward semantic redesign: use learned or retrieved shape priors. It is not a drop-in image-to-16px converter and does not establish an optimum for this corpus.

The Pixel Joint tutorial by cure emphasizes coherent pixel clusters and warns that excess dithering or isolated pixels can become distracting noise. It also recognizes essential one-pixel details. That supports scoring regions rather than indiscriminately deleting all isolated marks.

Best practical approach for these inputs

  1. Start from source support. Alpha supplies the outer shape and real transparent gaps. Do not make dark source pixels transparent just because they are dark. Rejecting a black outline is a separate operation, based on a consistent dark border adjoining a brighter body.
  2. Trim cautiously. Peel only a confidently inferred drawn border. Protect thin shafts, fingers, blade tips, and each meaningful source component. A uniform erosion is destructive at this resolution. Near-black border tests should consider RGB Value as well as luma so saturated blue is not automatically treated as black.
  3. Separate shading from candidate structure. Test dark valleys with brighter samples on opposite sides; smooth monotonic gradients are weak evidence. Color boundaries can supply independent support, but normalized chromaticity is only a shading cue: hue-shifted AI shadows violate a simple multiplicative-lighting model.
  4. Score short regions, not independent pixels. Join supported candidate grooves in their dominant direction; prefer a small number of coherent marks. Penalize scattered cuts, checker texture, thick dark masses, redundant parallel features, and cuts that consume a disproportionate fraction of a tiny item.
  5. Constrain the result. Protect foreground connectivity while allowing deliberate new interior holes. Use a detail budget per object; component-count, hole-count, thin-part survival and contour changes are separate checks. A local foreground-connectivity guard alone is not full topology preservation.
  6. Choose stable detail levels. Stronger, accepted details should remain when Detail increases. Prefer gradual candidate addition to a threshold-triggered all-or-nothing fallback.

The earlier prototype improves border handling, but still turns armor texture into scattered holes in several supplied items. The breastplate body also remains visually generic: brightness/color statistics do not reliably identify its neckline, shoulder boundaries, or chest form. This is a concrete remaining failure, not evidence that palette size must be restricted.

How to judge improvements

Keep the supplied breastplate plus all 64 sheet items as the fixed test corpus. Compare at true 16×16 and nearest-neighbor magnification, with both light and dark backgrounds. Measure empty items, extra ink components, lost source gaps, contour displacement, surviving thin parts, isolated new cuts, and percentage of body removed. Check sprite-sheet versus individually cropped items and rotation/mirror consistency.

Those are guardrails, not recognition scores. A solid blob scores perfectly on connectivity. The deciding test is blinded identification of object categories and preference among candidate outputs, without showing filenames or the source first. Avoid tuning solely for the known breastplate. No cited algorithm proves that the most recognizable result can be recovered from arbitrary AI shading at 16×16; a claim of optimality would need task-specific recognition data.

Implementation recommendation: ship the conservative structural body + sparse coherent detail approach, retaining an outline override and a stable Detail slider. Treat graph-cut base/detail optimization as the next controlled comparator if visual checks still expose scattered texture. Semantic template or learned iconification is a separate capability with different dependencies and identity risks.
