# Colorfetti identity and copy concepts

October 13, 2026. Colorfetti is the chosen brand name for the browser-based image, sprite and animated GIF recoloring tool. Use a capital C and lowercase f everywhere. The strongest proposed pairing is **Confetti C** with **Small pieces. Big color.**, supported by a clear description of recoloring.

The name blends color and confetti. Its useful creative connection is that tiny pieces become an image, while a palette determines their colors. Confetti belongs in the identifying shape and in the language about color; the app should still be understood as creative software. Trademark clearance remains unresolved. These are proposal assets, not a deployed app rebrand.

## Recommended brand presentation

**Colorfetti**  
**Small pieces. Big color.**  
Recolor images, sprites, and animated GIFs with automatic, preset, or custom palettes. Explore two-color styles for a different look.

Use **Image and GIF recoloring tool** when a short category label is needed. A separate privacy line can say **Images stay on your device.** Confetti-themed copy should never be the only explanation of the function.

| Option | Intent and limitation |
| --- | --- |
| **Colorful pieces. Fresh perspectives.** | Strong confetti/pixel connection with room beyond pixel art; function needs explanation. |
| Pass your pixels a fresh palette. | Alliteration and transformation. Best paired with Palette relay. |

“Confetti your images” and “Sprinkle confetti onto your GIFs” are poor primary lines because they imply adding decorative particles. “Keep every detail” is also unsuitable: color limits, cleanup and two-color styles can remove detail. The small-pieces metaphor should communicate a different color treatment, not a claim that the image is cut apart or new pixels are generated.

## Four distinct logo directions

The [comparison board](colorfetti-concepts/colorfetti-directions.png) shows original pixel geometry, a wordmark combination, an app-icon presentation, a monochrome presentation and small sizes for every direction. Each concept has its own identifying structure; they are not color variants of one symbol.

### Confetti C

A chunky stepped C is built from three broad color pieces, with two detached square fragments on its open side. Confetti forms the letter itself instead of decorating a generic logo. The initial connects the symbol to Colorfetti, and the open letter remains clear when every part uses the same color.

**Pairing:** Small pieces. Big color.

**Strength:** Best balance of brand recognition, pixel character and a restrained amount of play. A clean, single-color wordmark keeps the combination appropriate for creative software. The fragments can become a repeatable visual motif for illustrations and transitions.

**Risk:** Multicolor letterforms are a crowded territory. Keep the chunky stepped construction and fixed fragment positions; avoid turning it into a smooth rainbow ring or adding arbitrary confetti everywhere.

### Confetti canvas

An open pixel frame holds separate palette fragments, with one square occupying the opening at the upper-right. The frame suggests existing artwork, and the colored pieces suggest its palette. This gives confetti a structural relationship to the app's function.

**Pairing:** A fresh palette for every pixel.

**Strength:** Clearest software/tool association. Good when immediate understanding matters more than the celebratory side of the name.

**Risk:** The small frame and interior pieces create more visual complexity. The prototype is readable as a shape at 16 pixels, but a dedicated favicon variant could simplify the interior if real-device tests require it. It can resemble a generic image or layout app without the wordmark.

### Palette relay

Three overlapping square swatches form a diagonal path, with two detached fragments echoing their corners. The color changes along the path express passing an image through different palette choices. It is a compact transformation motif rather than a letter or frame.

**Pairing:** Pass your pixels a fresh palette.

**Strength:** Most directly about palette change. The linked squares remain a distinctive diagonal silhouette in one color; the extra fragments connect the construction to confetti.

**Risk:** Could suggest layers, linked objects or a generic design system. It carries less explicit recognition of Colorfetti than the C. Its meaning needs the description.

### Confetti burst

Six separated chunky pieces orbit a central square. The symbol is deliberately built from scattered pixel confetti, without a letter, frame or chain of swatches.

**Pairing:** Give your colors something to celebrate.

**Strength:** Most playful and most literal connection to confetti. Useful as a campaign motif, a loading sequence or a supporting illustration.

**Risk:** Strongest party-brand association and weakest recoloring signal. In monochrome it can read as a sun or activity indicator. It is the least suitable primary identity of these four, despite its expressive potential.

## Wordmarks and application icons

All four wordmark combinations use **Colorfetti** with the exact requested capitalization. A calm sans-serif wordmark balances the bright pixel symbols. The lettering is a typographic sketch using Segoe UI with an Arial fallback; convert the chosen lettering to outlines or use an appropriately licensed bundled font before final production.

Each direction includes a transparent symbol, a same-geometry monochrome symbol, color and monochrome wordmark combinations, and standalone square 512px application icons. The comparison board's rounded containers illustrate typical application presentation; the exported app files themselves use a full square background so platform masks can control their corners.

The working grid is 16 by 16 logical pixels. All mark geometry is integer-aligned, uses solid fills, and is free of gradients or blur. Small PNG samples are supplied at 16, 24, 32 and 48px. The 24px samples use nearest-neighbor scaling; fractional grid enlargement can produce uneven pixel widths, so it remains a visual review size rather than proof that one grid fits every rendering condition.

Automated geometry checks confirm binary alpha, unchanged monochrome silhouettes and that every identifying pixel in the 512px app exports fits within the centered safe circle of radius 204.8px. These checks establish geometric properties, not measured user recognition. Color and monochrome samples also need inspection in the actual app, browser tabs, installation surfaces and phone display scaling.

The mark should use a fixed brand palette across Help, favicon, README, social previews and installed icons. A live demo can recolor the same geometry while clearly remaining example artwork. After choosing one direction, derive every public asset from that master; do not use a different concept for each surface.

## Recommended pairing and next decision

Choose **Confetti C + Small pieces. Big color.** as the first direction to refine. Its confetti pieces form the brand initial, its silhouette survives monochrome, and its simple structure gives it the strongest starting point for small icons.

Choose **Confetti canvas + A fresh palette for every pixel.** if user testing shows that the function needs to be more obvious at first glance. Test both with the same balanced app description so the visual comparison does not confuse logo preference with comprehension.

Refinement should concentrate on the C's spacing and fragment placement, native favicon appearance, final wordmark lettering and application mockups. The chosen direction can then drive the live rebrand. Source concepts and renders are editable; no current app branding or persistence keys were changed by this concept work.
